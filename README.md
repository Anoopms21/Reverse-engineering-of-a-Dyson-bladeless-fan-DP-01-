# Reverse Engineering & Design Optimization of Dyson DP-01 Bladeless Fan

### Mechanical Product Design | Reverse Engineering | DFM/DFA | Cost Optimization

**University of Colorado Boulder | Design for Manufacturability – Project 1**

**August – October 2024**

---

## Project Overview

This project focuses on reverse engineering and redesigning the Dyson DP-01 bladeless fan to improve manufacturability, assembly efficiency, and production cost.

The original product was analyzed through component-level reverse engineering, CAD reconstruction, material selection, manufacturing process evaluation, and Design for Manufacturing and Assembly (DFM/DFA).

The redesigned architecture achieved significant reductions in estimated manufacturing costs, material costs, and fastener complexity while improving assembly efficiency.

## Project Objectives

- Reverse engineer the original Dyson DP-01 product architecture.
- Develop detailed component and assembly CAD models.
- Identify manufacturing and assembly inefficiencies.
- Optimize material selection and manufacturing processes.
- Reduce production costs and component complexity.
- Improve the overall Design for Assembly (DFA) score.

---

## Reverse Engineering & CAD Development

- Reverse-engineered **41 components and 7 assemblies** in SolidWorks by reconstructing component geometry, mating interfaces, and assembly architecture.
- Evaluated material selection and manufacturing feasibility for individual components.
- Developed an integrated CAD assembly to support design analysis and optimization.

## Design for Manufacturing (DFM)

The redesign focused on simplifying production requirements through material and manufacturing process optimization.

- Reduced estimated manufacturing costs by **14.3%** through component redesign and manufacturing process consolidation.
- Reduced estimated raw material costs by **12.8%** through material selection and product architecture optimization.
- Consolidated the redesigned product into **4 materials and 4 corresponding manufacturing processes**.
- Achieved projected manufacturing savings of **$14 per unit**, equivalent to **$140,000 at 10,000 units**.

## Design for Assembly (DFA)

Assembly optimization focused on reducing fastening complexity and improving product assembly efficiency.

- Reduced unique fastener types from **18 to 12**, achieving a **33.3% reduction** through hardware standardization.
- Improved the overall DFA score by **13.8%** through product architecture simplification and assembly optimization.
- Evaluated component interfaces, assembly sequence, and fastening requirements.
